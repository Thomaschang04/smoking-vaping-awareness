# Disadvantages of Smoking and Vaping
## Detailed Presentation Script with Malaysian Examples

**Suggested duration:** 7–10 minutes  
**Audience:** School or college presentation  
**Topic:** Three major disadvantages of smoking and vaping

---

## Introduction

Good morning everyone.

Today, I am going to talk about the disadvantages of smoking and vaping. These habits are sometimes treated as ordinary or harmless, especially when people see them frequently in public or on social media. However, both smoking and vaping can expose people to substances that may harm their health. They can also create financial pressure and affect the people around the user.

Before discussing the disadvantages, it is important to understand the difference between the two. Traditional cigarettes burn tobacco and produce smoke containing nicotine, tar, carbon monoxide and many other harmful chemicals. Vapes, on the other hand, heat a liquid to produce an aerosol. This aerosol is not simply harmless water vapour. It may contain nicotine, fine particles, flavouring chemicals and other substances. The contents and risks can vary between products.

For this presentation, I will focus on three main disadvantages:
1. Damage to health and risk of addiction.
2. Financial burden on individuals and families.
3. Harm to other people and the wider community.

## 1. Damage to Health and Risk of Addiction

The first and most important disadvantage is the effect of smoking and vaping on human health.

Cigarette smoking is a major cause of preventable disease. Tobacco smoke contains thousands of chemicals, including substances that can damage body cells. Long-term smoking increases the risk of serious illnesses such as lung cancer, heart disease, stroke and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, which makes breathing increasingly difficult. Smoking can also reduce physical fitness, cause persistent coughing and affect a person's ability to exercise or carry out daily activities.

For example, imagine a Malaysian father who has smoked for many years. At first, he may only notice that he coughs in the morning or becomes breathless when climbing stairs. Over time, these symptoms may become more serious and require repeated medical appointments or hospital treatment. His illness can affect not only his own quality of life but also his ability to work and support his family.

Vaping should not be considered risk-free either. Many vape liquids contain nicotine, which is addictive. Nicotine can lead to dependence, meaning a person may feel strong urges to use the product and find it difficult to stop. Nicotine exposure is particularly concerning for adolescents because the brain is still developing. Vaping aerosols can also contain substances that irritate the lungs or may have other harmful effects. Scientists are still studying the long-term effects of vaping because these products are relatively new compared with conventional cigarettes.

A relevant example in Malaysia is a student who starts vaping because friends say it is fashionable or because the flavours seem attractive. The student may initially use it only during gatherings. However, if the product contains nicotine, repeated use can lead to dependence. The student may then find it difficult to concentrate on schoolwork when experiencing cravings, or may spend money that should have been used for meals, transport or learning materials.

It is also important to understand that switching from cigarettes to vapes does not make vaping harmless. The health risks are not identical, and the level of risk depends on the product and pattern of use. However, people who do not smoke should not start vaping, and young people should avoid both products.

**Transition:** Apart from affecting health, smoking and vaping can also create a financial burden that may be underestimated.

## 2. Financial Burden on Individuals and Families

The second disadvantage is the amount of money spent on smoking and vaping.

Some people think that buying one cigarette pack or one vape product at a time is a small expense. However, when the cost is repeated every day or every week, the total can become significant. Money spent on these products could instead be used for food, education, transport, savings or household needs.

For example, consider a person who spends RM15 a day on smoking-related products. Over 30 days, that adds up to RM450. Over one year, the total would be RM5,475. This is only an illustration; actual spending depends on the product, price and frequency of use. Nevertheless, it shows how a seemingly small daily expense can become a large annual cost.

For a Malaysian household, RM450 a month could help pay utility bills, buy groceries, cover school expenses or contribute to savings. If a parent spends a substantial amount on cigarettes or vape products, the whole family may feel the impact, especially when the household budget is already tight.

There are also indirect costs. Smoking-related illnesses may lead to medical expenses, time away from work and reduced income. Family members may need to spend time caring for someone who is unwell. Even when treatment is available through the public healthcare system, illness can still create costs through transport, time off work and other household arrangements.

Vaping can also involve repeated spending on devices, replacement parts, liquids or disposable products, depending on the type used. Because the spending may happen in small amounts, users may not notice how quickly it accumulates.

A simple Malaysian example would be a young person who receives pocket money for the school week. If part of that money is regularly spent on vaping, there may be less left for lunch, books or transport. This can create unnecessary financial pressure and may also lead to conflict with parents or guardians.

**Transition:** The effects do not stop with the person who smokes or vapes. These habits can also affect family members, other members of the public and the environment.

## 3. Harm to Other People and the Wider Community

The third disadvantage is the impact on people nearby and on the community.

One major concern is second-hand smoke. This is the smoke that other people breathe when someone smokes nearby. It contains harmful substances, and non-smokers can be exposed without choosing to smoke themselves. Children, older people, pregnant women and people with existing breathing or heart conditions may be especially vulnerable.

For example, imagine a family living in an apartment in Kuala Lumpur, Kuching or another Malaysian city. If someone regularly smokes on a balcony or near an open window, smoke may drift into neighbouring homes or shared spaces. Children or other family members may breathe in the smoke even though they do not smoke. This makes smoking a concern not only for the smoker but also for the people sharing the same environment.

Vaping can also affect people nearby. The aerosol released by a vape is not just clean air or water vapour. It can contain nicotine and other substances, and bystanders may breathe in some of the aerosol. The exact exposure varies according to the product and the surroundings, but vaping in shared indoor spaces can still affect other people.

Another community concern is litter and waste. Cigarette filters are commonly discarded on roadsides, outside shops, at bus stops and near beaches. They can contribute to pollution and are difficult to break down. Improperly discarded vape devices and cartridges can also create waste, including electronic components and batteries that should be handled appropriately rather than thrown carelessly into the environment.

Think about a popular Malaysian public area such as a food court, a park or a beach. If visitors leave cigarette butts or vape-related waste behind, cleaners and local authorities have to spend additional time and resources managing the litter. It also makes the area less pleasant for families, tourists and other visitors.

Smoking and vaping can therefore become more than an individual choice. They can affect public comfort, cleanliness and the well-being of people who share the same spaces.

## Conclusion

In conclusion, smoking and vaping have three major disadvantages.

First, they can harm health. Smoking is strongly linked to serious diseases, while vaping is not harmless and can expose users to nicotine and other potentially harmful substances. Nicotine dependence is a particular concern for young people.

Second, they can create a financial burden. Regular spending can add up to hundreds or even thousands of ringgit a year, while smoking-related illness may create additional costs for individuals and families.

Third, they can affect other people and the wider community through second-hand smoke, exposure to vape aerosol and environmental waste.

As members of Malaysian society, we should make informed decisions, avoid starting these habits and support friends or family members who want to stop. We should also respect shared spaces and the health of people around us.

The key message is simple: **the consequences of smoking and vaping are not limited to the user. They can affect health, family finances and the community as a whole.**

Thank you.
